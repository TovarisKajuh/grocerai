What we are building

Eat well. Pay less. Never plan again.

The user answers five questions once. Every Thursday they get a week of meals built around their goal, a grocery list priced at every store nearby, the cheapest trip, and the euro saving against the store they already shop at.

Highlighted: the part nobody else does. Meal planners stop at the list; price apps do not know what you will eat.

The number the product lives on

“−13,40 € this week vs. Mercator only.” Always against the user's own usual store, so it means something to them.

The rhythm

Weekly, keyed to supermarket leaflets. Thursday: new prices, new plan, one push notification.

Key decisions in the plan

Five choices that shape everything.

  • 1
    Expo mobile app, TypeScript
    Phone-first product with app-store presence. One codebase for iOS, Android and the web demo. Same language as the backend.
  • 2
    One engine, runs on the phone and the server
    The prototype's planner and optimiser become a shared package. Instant interactions on-device; a server run for the Thursday push with a real number.
  • 3
    Modular monolith on managed EU infrastructure
    Supabase (Postgres, auth, storage) + Fly.io. No Kubernetes, no microservices. Under 100 € a month through beta.
  • 4
    Python price pipeline with a human in the loop
    Scrape sites and leaflets weekly, match the ~40 recipe products per chain with embeddings + LLM, review only the uncertain ones.
  • 5
    Local-first: useful before the backend exists
    Phase 1 is the whole prototype as a real app on sample prices, no account. We validate the loop while the hard data work runs.

Each of these is a row in decisions.xlsx with the alternatives we weighed. None is irreversible before phase 2; the first two are the expensive ones to change after it.

The moat and the risk

The hard part is price data.

Every number in the product depends on knowing what roughly forty products cost at five chains, every week. Nobody sells us that feed today.

Why it is hard

  • Lidl and Hofer publish leaflets, not price lists
  • Every chain names and packs the same product differently
  • Sites change; scrapers break silently
  • Terms of service differ per chain; legal exposure is real
  • A wrong match means a wrong price and a wrong saving

What we do about it

  • Scope to the recipe products, not whole catalogs
  • One isolated adapter per chain; raw snapshots kept, so a parser bug never means a re-fetch
  • LLM-assisted matching with a review queue; the matching memory shrinks the work each week
  • Stale fallback: a broken chain carries last week's prices, the week still builds
  • Legal review per chain before phase 2; retailer partnerships pursued in parallel
≥ 90 %
The KPI the pipeline runs on. Coverage: recipe products with a live price at each enabled chain, every week. Below it, we alert; the app shows “prices from last week”.

Risks

Risks, and how the plan absorbs them.

RiskImpactMitigation
Price data access: legal terms, site changesHighLegal review per chain; isolated adapters with raw snapshots; stale fallback; partnerships in parallel
Product matching qualityHighScope to ~40 recipe products; review queue for low confidence; coverage and price-move alerts
Recipe corpus too small, weeks repeatMediumVariety scoring in the planner; corpus to 100+ before beta with dietitian review
Health data compliance (GDPR Art. 9)HighExplicit consent, EU-only hosting, skippable body metrics, export and delete before the first external user
Savings claims not credibleHighAlways vs. the user's own usual store; receipts collected in beta; savings shown compared with recorded spend
Two-developer bandwidthMediumPhase 1 ships value with no backend; managed infrastructure; module ownership keeps the two streams from blocking

The first two are the ones to watch: they are the moat, and they are the only risks the team cannot engineer away alone. The legal review and the retailer conversations need to start in phase 1, not phase 2.
